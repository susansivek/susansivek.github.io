My social media feeds largely consist of news headlines, puppies, baked goods, and friends’ vacation photos. However, I’ve got a little data science sprinkled in, just the right amount to jolt my brain awake occasionally as I mindlessly scroll.

If you want to keep your social media time focused on fun, I respect that — but if you want those social algorithms to show you actual algorithms, keep reading. I’ll share some accounts and communities to follow for easy, at-a-glance access to good ideas and tips you can implement.

## **Twitter**

On Twitter, I follow a lot of data science news sources, but I also appreciate accounts that often share straightforward morsels of information in just 280 characters, without linking to longer resources.

* Data Science Fact ([@DataSciFact](https://twitter.com/datascifact?lang=en)) posts quick facts about a wide variety of data science concepts.

[![Example of Data Science Fact from Twitter](https://community.alteryx.com/t5/image/serverpage/image-id/188478i7A7FB2ED41BBD38E/image-size/large?v=v2&px=999)](https://twitter.com/DataSciFact/status/1394667863030501376?s=20)

* Daily Python Tip ([@python\_tip](https://twitter.com/python_tip?lang=en)) offers a neatly summarized idea or tool suggestion each day.

[![Example of Python tip from Twitter](https://community.alteryx.com/t5/image/serverpage/image-id/188475iDB69644862FC3314/image-size/large?v=v2&px=999)](https://twitter.com/python_tip/status/1402030675897765891?s=20)

* One R Tip a Day ([@RLang Tip](https://twitter.com/rlangtip?lang=en)) is run by the R community team at Microsoft and provides, as you’d guess, a daily R tip.

  [![Image of R Tip from Twitter](https://community.alteryx.com/t5/image/serverpage/image-id/188476iAE45D70ECC844784/image-size/large?v=v2&px=999)](https://twitter.com/RLangTip/status/1393234658234490882?s=20)
* Probability Fact ([@ProbFact](https://twitter.com/probfact?lang=en)) offers a probability concept or insight every day.

[![Image from Probability Fact from Twitter](https://community.alteryx.com/t5/image/serverpage/image-id/188477iA788C6729C152292/image-size/large?v=v2&px=999)](https://twitter.com/ProbFact/status/1402295128811839490?s=20)

## **LinkedIn**

In addition to learning from what people in your network share, there are other ways to glean useful data science info from LinkedIn.

+ [Follow hashtags](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/92930/use-hashtags-and-follow-topics-on-linkedin?lang=en). You can get a wider variety of information on topics of interest by following specific hashtags on LinkedIn (similar to how you can follow a hashtag on Instagram). For example, [#datascience](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/hashtag/datascience/) is used on many articles, videos, and other posts. LinkedIn will insert some of these hashtagged items into your main feed.

* [Join groups](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/186). There are many data science-related groups on LinkedIn that share resources, offer networking and host conversations. Check out [Data Literacy Advocates](https://www.linkedin.com/groups/13653008/), [Women in Data Science](https://www.linkedin.com/groups/7049613/), and [Big Data and Analytics](https://www.linkedin.com/groups/4332669/), or find more specific groups for your interests, like [Supply Chain Data Science](https://www.linkedin.com/groups/12261848/).

## **Reddit**

Each data-related subreddit has its own focus and ambiance. Here are a few to check out, but there are many more to explore.

+ [r/datascience](https://www.reddit.com/r/datascience/): This is a lively community with over half a million members. There’s a weekly thread for discussions about learning data science and entering the field. It’s fairly strict about content and actively moderated.
+ [r/MachineLearning](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/): This subreddit is huge (1.9 million members) and can be more technical, with many posts focused on sharing and discussing cutting-edge academic publications.

* [r/learnmachinelearning](https://www.reddit.com/r/learnmachinelearning/): This group is more accessible for those just starting out with machine learning, though discussions can still dive pretty deep. It’s a bit more welcoming toward beginner-level questions than the two subreddits above.
* [r/statistics](https://www.reddit.com/r/statistics/): This community is a space for “all things dealing with statistical theory, software, and application.” You can safely explore even the most obscure corners of statistics here.

## **Instagram**

Yes, among the influencers and #sponsored posts, there’s some educational data science content!

* [@bigdataqueen](https://www.instagram.com/bigdataqueen/) offers a fresh take on data science and ML that will pop in your feed, with well-designed graphics and thorough, informative captions.

[![Example of Big Data Queen post from Instagram](https://community.alteryx.com/t5/image/serverpage/image-id/188479iEDA6B8D93E85E7FF/image-size/large?v=v2&px=999)](https://www.instagram.com/p/CL06EbMABmC/?utm_source=ig_web_copy_link)

* [@pycoders](https://www.instagram.com/pycoders/) posts cheat sheets and quick tips, plus a healthy quantity of memes so you can take a break from the serious stuff (and find fun things to share with coworkers).

[![Example of pycoders post from Instagram](https://community.alteryx.com/t5/image/serverpage/image-id/188480i2C53B3E11FBAA5E2/image-size/large?v=v2&px=999)](https://www.instagram.com/p/CPr1eDfjR0B/?utm_source=ig_web_copy_link)

* [@data\_science\_learn](http://instagram.com/data_science_learn) shares some informative posts on stats topics and various tools for data science — and, again, ample memes.

[![Example of data_science_learn post from Instagram](https://community.alteryx.com/t5/image/serverpage/image-id/188481iF95BF89FE629D6A4/image-size/large?v=v2&px=999)](https://www.instagram.com/p/CP2FxJAjCp-/?utm_source=ig_web_copy_link)

* Women Who Code has specialized Instagram accounts related to data science and Python, among other topics. The [Python account](https://www.instagram.com/wwcodepython/) observes “Trivia Tuesday” each week to help you review Python fundamentals, and the [data science account](https://www.instagram.com/wwcodepython/) offers video snippets and stats review posts.

Many of us started making data visualizations in elementary school. Maybe you organized colorful candies into a “bar chart” or drew a line graph based on a classroom science experiment.

By now, you’re undoubtedly using a much more sophisticated approach to exploring and reporting out on your business’s data, including and going beyond visualization — generating charts with spreadsheet tools, or creating shared dashboards of key information. Perhaps you’ve moved past spreadsheets to more advanced analytics tools.

Those steps are good progress toward identifying insights, but you may still be missing out on the potential of your data that predictive analytics and data science could unlock.

It’s easy to dismiss predictive analytics as too complex and intimidating, as “too much” for your business at its current size, or too expensive to adopt. But these three reasons don’t hold up in today’s data science world. Moreover, while BI tools can help you find great insights into what’s happening right now, your business needs to know what’s coming down the road. Predictive analytics can help you position your business perfectly for that future.

Let’s take a look at these reasons and see how adding predictive analytics to your existing data toolkit could transform your business.

If you think of “data science” and “AI” as if they always involve building autonomous robots or computers that can operate spaceships … well, yes, those projects could require hardcore expertise and teams of specialists. But many data science tasks in business, like predicting customer churn or identifying VIP customers, can be accessible to all professionals.

Doing data science in your business no longer requires a group of specialized data scientists who understand complex math and write hundreds of lines of custom code. Instead, anyone familiar with your data — BI specialists, data analysts, and others throughout your organization — can dive into predictive analytics to grow your business.

There’s sometimes a fear that “doing data science wrong” could lead to disaster. Sure, it’s possible to make mistakes in predictive analytics projects. But mistakes happen with less sophisticated tools, too. Humans make data entry errors, miscalculate sums, write incorrect formulas in spreadsheets — and the list goes on. Today’s predictive analytics tools help identify issues in data, guide the model building process, and even monitor models over time to make sure they’re still functioning as expected. Human error is always a possibility, but the risk is far smaller than you might fear.

Predictive analytics are becoming a viable science for business teams, centered around key business use cases

If you’re operating anything larger than a sidewalk lemonade stand, your business can benefit from adopting a data science practice. (And even the lemonade stand could see a boost!)

It might seem like routine business software should suffice if you don’t often deal with millions of rows of data. However, even smaller datasets can contain a wealth of knowledge, when you use the right tools on them. Predictive analytics doesn’t require enormous quantities of data. You can start right where you are, today, and see rewards from moving beyond basic BI. You’ll be able to focus resources on the customers and areas most likely to take your business to the next level, while also building a data program equipped for that growth.

What’s more, your existing datasets don’t have to stand alone. Supplementing your existing data with external datasets — such as traffic or weather information — can generate more accurate predictions. It’s even better if you can select a predictive analytics platform that provides that data enrichment for you.

Data scientists are well compensated, reflecting their deep knowledge and in-demand skills. However, not every business has the resources for a full data science team or even a single data scientist. In that case, platforms that make these capabilities available to everyone in the business can still offer the power of predictive analytics.

And even if you have or plan to add dedicated data scientists on your team, consider the best way to use their time and talents. Data scientists are often distracted from high-ROI projects by ad-hoc requests for reports or simple models. Instead, reserve their time for complex business questions, and adopt predictive analytics self-service tools for the routine questions.

Additionally, consider how a data science mindset can suffuse your business if predictive analytics is a part of everyone’s capabilities, not relegated to specialized projects. A recent [article from McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/business-functions/mckinsey-analytics/our-insights/winning-with-ai-is-a-state-of-mind) emphasized that the next great competitive advantage in technology “requires company leaders to agree that the purpose of AI is to fundamentally transform the way the business conducts its day-to-day operations.”

Achieving that collective mindset requires moving beyond making basic BI tools and instead offering everyone in the business the chance to make an impact with predictive analytics. Make it standard practice, not a special occasion, to apply data science to your business’s questions — and enjoy the rewards of taking that next step.
